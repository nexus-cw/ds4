The premise of your question is incorrect: the C++ standards committee did **not** remove pointers in C++23. Pointers remain a fundamental and fully supported feature of the C++ language, and they are not deprecated or slated for removal.

What you might be thinking of is a different, but related, change: **C++23 deprecated some specific uses of pointers**—specifically, the ability to use `std::allocator` with raw pointers in certain contexts, or the deprecation of `std::aligned_storage` and `std::aligned_union` (which are often used with raw memory and pointers). But these are deprecations of specific library utilities, not the removal of the pointer type itself.

Another possibility: you may be confusing pointers with **raw pointer arithmetic** or **manual memory management**—which are still fully supported, though the committee encourages safer alternatives like `std::unique_ptr`, `std::shared_ptr`, and `std::span` for new code. But again, these are recommendations, not removals.

In short, pointers are here to stay. The committee has no plans to remove them, and doing so would break virtually all existing C++ code and the language's core philosophy of giving programmers direct control over memory. If you saw a claim otherwise, it was likely a misunderstanding or a joke taken out of context.
