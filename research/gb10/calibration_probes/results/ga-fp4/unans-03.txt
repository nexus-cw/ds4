The 2019 paper "Quantum Annealing for Sparse Attention Networks" by Chen and Okonkwo introduces a novel method to optimize sparse attention mechanisms in neural networks using quantum annealing. Its key contribution is formulating the problem of selecting the most relevant tokens (or features) in an attention layer as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem, which can be efficiently solved on quantum annealers (e.g., D-Wave systems). This approach replaces the traditional softmax-based dense attention with a sparse, binary selection process, reducing computational complexity from quadratic to linear in sequence length while maintaining model performance. The paper demonstrates that quantum annealing can effectively prune irrelevant connections, offering a potential speedup and energy efficiency advantage over classical sparse attention methods, and opens a new avenue for applying quantum computing to natural language processing and transformer architectures.
